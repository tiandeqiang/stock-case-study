### Case Overview

Dan Bin, founder of Oriental Harbor Investment, is one of China's most prominent value investors. Beginning in the early 2000s, Dan built a high-conviction position in **Kweichow Moutai**, China’s premier liquor producer.

Over the next two decades, Moutai faced severe macroeconomic headwinds, including the 2008 global financial crisis, the 2012 anti-extravagance government policies, and multiple market crashes. Through it all, Dan held firm, viewing Moutai as a **living textbook of durable competitive advantage**. His multi-decade holding generated exceptional compounding returns.

---

### Core Investment Thesis (Moat & Business Model)

1. **Zero Inventory Impairment & Negative Asset Decay**
   Unlike tech hardware or perishable fast-moving consumer goods that lose value over time, aged Sauce-flavor Baijiu improves with age. Moutai’s inventory actually appreciates in quality and market value, creating a rare business model with **zero inventory write-down risk**.

2. **Social Currency and Cultural Monopoly**
   Moutai transcends being a beverage; it acts as a primary "social currency" and status symbol in business networking and premium gift-giving in China. This intangible cultural lock-in grants the company immense **pricing power**.

3. **Geographical and Environmental Capacity Bottleneck**
   Authentic Moutai production is environmentally constrained to the specific microclimate, water source, and microbial ecosystem of Moutai Town along the Chishui River. This geographical limitation caps supply and creates a perpetual structural shortage.

### Key Takeaways & Investment Philosophy

> **Dan Bin's Insight**: *"Look for businesses that compound with time rather than those threatened by rapid technological disruption. Time is the ultimate moat."*

* **Disruption Resistance**: High-tech innovators face constant existential threats from newer technologies. In contrast, iconic cultural consumer brands enjoy long business lifespans with minimal reinvestment risk.
* **Conviction Under Fire**: The true test of value investing is not finding great companies, but having the intellectual fortitude to hold them through severe market downturns and negative news cycles.
