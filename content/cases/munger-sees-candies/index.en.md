### Case Overview

In 1972, driven by Charlie Munger’s insistence, Berkshire Hathaway acquired **See's Candies**, a California-based boxed-chocolate maker, for a net purchase price of **$25 million**.

At the time, See's had only $8 million in net tangible assets. Warren Buffett was initially reluctant to pay such a high premium, but Munger recognized the company's unshakeable consumer loyalty and immense pricing power. To date, See's Candies has generated over **$2 billion** in cumulative pre-tax profits for Berkshire, serving as the financial engine that funded future iconic investments like Coca-Cola and Apple.

---

### Core Investment Thesis (Moat & Business Model)

1. **Unrivaled Pricing Power**
   See's held an emotional monopoly in California for holiday gifting. Munger realized the company could raise prices every single year without losing customers to cheaper alternatives.

2. **Exceptional ROIC with Minimal Capital Reinvestment**
   Unlike industrial manufacturers, See's required very little incremental capital to grow. Nearly all of its net income converted directly into free cash flow, which Berkshire redirected into higher-return opportunities.

3. **Paradigm Shift for Berkshire Hathaway**
   This deal marked Munger's decisive influence in breaking Buffett away from Ben Graham's "cigar-butt" value investing style: **"It is far better to buy a wonderful company at a fair price than a fair company at a wonderful price."**

---

### Key Takeaways & Mental Models

> **Munger's Insight**: *"If we hadn't bought See's Candies, we wouldn't have bought Coca-Cola. See's taught us to appreciate the power of intangible assets and brand moats."*

* **Rule of Thumb**: When analyzing a business, look beyond book value and tangible assets. Sustainable competitive advantage often lies in **Share of Mind** and intangible brand equity.
